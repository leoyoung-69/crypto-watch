## Alpha / ideas to research (not financial advice)

1. **BTC's ETF cost basis (~$81.5k) is now overhead supply.** Glassnode (via Milk Road): ETF holders' average cost is ~$81.5k and treasury companies' ~$80.2k. Underwater cost bases tend to cap rallies, because holders sell into breakeven. Two clean levels follow: a daily close back above ~$81.5–82k means the ETF crowd is in profit again (bullish), while a sustained break of ~$80.2k puts treasury companies underwater too (bearish, and it adds pressure on Strategy, see #2). Trade the reclaim or the break, not the chop in between.
2. **Strategy (MSTR) is defending its preferred instead of buying BTC.** It spent $193.1M buying back STRC versus $22.1M (267 BTC) on bitcoin in the week to Oct 18, raised the buyback cap to $3B, and its USD cash fell from $640.5M to $447.3M in a week. STRC issuance, its main BTC-buying engine, is halted. Two catalysts: the **Oct 28 shareholder vote** on daily STRC dividends, and **MSCI's November review**, which could drop MSTR (and Metaplanet) from its indexes. The largest corporate BTC buyer is effectively paused, which removes a bid just as BTC tests the treasury cost basis. If you hold MSTR or STRC, watch the cash burn rate weekly.
3. **"Quality" alts are finally working; screen for it.** Jamie Coutts (Real Vision, via Milk Road) backtested the top 200 tokens: the highest-quality third (real fees, tight supply, value returned to holders) was −50% from 2022 while BTC was +82%, but in 2026 it's **+49% YTD versus BTC −6%**. He moved from 80–85% BTC to ~30–35% L1s and ~20% DeFi. That matches the holder-revenue screen from Oct 24 (RAY, AERO, UNI; avoid HYPE's unlock overhang). It's one year of data, so size accordingly, but the regime shift is consistent across three independent sources now.
4. **ETH: oversold is close, but don't front-run.** Daily RSI ~34–38 with positive funding means longs haven't capitulated yet. A flush toward RSI <30 with funding going negative would be the higher-quality long entry, especially with JPM Coin moving to Ethereum mainnet as the "public chains are safe for banks" proof point (Milk Road / Ethereum Institutional). Until then, the Bitmine buyer gap and ETF outflows from Oct 24 still dominate. A short is only worth considering if $2,330 fails on volume.
5. **GPU rents are rising while neocloud stocks fall.** H100 rental prices are up ~15% ($2.71 → $3.12/hr) since Sept 30, while CoreWeave, Nebius, IREN and Cipher are down 27–44% (Milk Road PRO). Meta says it lacks compute to launch Muse globally. That divergence supports the GPU-collateral theme from Oct 24 (USD.AI, CME GPU futures, CFTC review ends Nov 9) and the BTC-miner-turned-AI-host trade (IREN, Cipher). Milk Road PRO's analyst likes Nebius ~$172 (target $250–300) but expects most neoclouds to fail.
6. **Stablecoin-rewards rule = charter arbitrage.** Chain Catalyst's read: the OCC went first with the loosest rewards stance, so if the Fed/FDIC write tighter rules, issuers will shop for OCC charters. The American Bankers Association is already attacking it. Coinbase earned $292.1M in stablecoin revenue last quarter (~¼ of net revenue) on USDC rewards that now sit in a gray zone with no safe harbor. Treat that revenue line as a regulatory-risk item, not a moat.
7. **Security regime: more hacks, smaller each.** Dragonfly's Haseeb Qureshi: on-chain hack *frequency* is up vertically but total stolen is flat versus 2025, so it's a "mass looting" of small, poorly maintained protocols. Practical rule: keep size out of small or long-unaudited protocols and front-ends. That applies with extra force during the PHP/Node/Railway "Black October" wave covered Oct 21–24.
8. **Avoid / risk:**

   - **World Liberty / USD1:** a derivative suit alleges UAE-linked emoluments and estimates the MGX–Binance USD1 deal costs Circle $70–82M a year. Headline risk for USD1 and WLFI.
